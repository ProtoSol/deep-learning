8.5 Music Synthesis

Created Date: 2025-06-01

Music transcription, the previous page, turns a recording into notes. Synthesis runs the other direction: notes become sound. The symbolic side of that path in this repository is MIDI. A waveform model is not trained here.

8.5.1 MIDI as the score

A MIDI file is a timed list of events. Note-on and note-off carry a pitch, a velocity, and a timestamp in ticks. Tempo and time-signature events turn ticks into seconds. That list is what a transformer transcription model is trained to emit, and it is what a synthesizer consumes. midi_parse.py downloads a zip of MIDI files and extracts it under data/. It does not parse events itself. After it runs, any standard MIDI reader can list the note-ons.

8.5.2 From events to a waveform

A sampler plays a recorded note for each note-on and stops it at note-off. A parametric synthesizer does the same with an oscillator, an envelope, and a filter, one voice per sounding pitch. A neural synthesizer replaces the oscillator with a network conditioned on the pitch, the velocity, and the instrument. The text-to-speech page is the speech version of this last idea: tokens in, samples out. Music needs a pitch and a duration on each token, which speech tokens do not carry.

This folder stops at the download. To hear a file, open one of the extracted MIDI files in a sampler you already have. Training a neural instrument would be a new project, and the transcription chapter is the place that already learns the mapping from audio back onto these events.
