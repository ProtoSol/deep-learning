2.7 Training that Generalizes

Created Date: 2026-10-09

The optimizer page updates the weights. This page is why a deep stack can be updated at all, and why a low training loss is not the same as a model that works on new data. The same four ideas show up in the convnet, the transformer, and the language-model pages: the scale of the initial weights, a normalization inside each block, a penalty that keeps the fit from memorizing the batch, and a split of the data so the number you report was not used to choose the model.

2.7.1 Initialization

A linear layer multiplies the incoming activation by a weight matrix. If every entry of that matrix is drawn with standard deviation 1, each output is a sum of many large terms. A ReLU then keeps the positive half. Stack that a dozen times and the activations overflow. The backward pass multiplies the same scales, so the gradient vanishes or explodes with them.

He initialization (He et al.) draws the weights of a ReLU layer at

\(\mathrm{std} = \sqrt{2 / \mathrm{fan\_in}}\)

where \(\mathrm{fan\_in}\) is the number of inputs to the layer. The 2 pays for the ReLU throwing away the negative half, so the variance of the activation stays near the variance it came in with. Xavier initialization (Glorot and Bengio) is the same correction for a tanh layer, which does not discard half the values, so the 2 is absent.

train_stability.py runs both draws through 16 ReLU layers of width 64. The He stack finishes finite, with a standard deviation of order 1. The scale-1 stack does not. 

he_scale = numpy.sqrt(2.0 / width)
he_h = relu_stack(he_scale, depth, width, batch, rng)
unit_h = relu_stack(1.0, depth, width, batch, rng)

2.7.2 Normalization

Initialization sets the scale at step 0. Training moves the weights, and the next layer then sees a distribution it was not initialized for. Batch normalization (Ioffe and Szegedy) recenters and rescales each channel using the mean and variance of the current minibatch. At evaluation time those two numbers are the running averages stored during training.

A transformer token does not share a batch axis with its neighbors in the same way a convnet channel does. Layer normalization (Ba, Kiros, and Hinton) computes the mean and variance across the features of one token. That is the LayerNorm the transformer blocks already call. RMSNorm drops the mean shift and divides by the root mean square of the features. The residual connection on the ResNet page is why a normalized block can be stacked: the block learns a change, and the skip carries the previous scale through.

2.7.3 Regularization

Dropout (Srivastava et al.) zeros each unit with probability \(p\) during training and multiplies the survivors by \(1/(1-p)\), so the expected activation matches the activation at test time, when no units are dropped. The network cannot rely on one co-adapted feature.

Weight decay is the \(\lambda \theta\) term already written on the AdamW section. It pulls each weight toward zero on its own step, separate from the adaptive update. Early stopping watches a held-out loss and keeps the checkpoint from before that loss starts to rise. The weights that minimize the training loss are not the weights you ship.

On a GPU, torch.amp runs the forward pass in fp16 or bf16 and scales the loss so the small gradients do not underflow when they are cast back. The update rule is unchanged.

2.7.4 The split and the first debug

Train, validation, and test are three jobs. The training rows update the weights. The validation rows choose the checkpoint, the learning rate, and the width. The test rows are reported once, after those choices are frozen. A number computed on data that was used to choose the model is a training number.

The first debug is smaller than a new architecture. Take a handful of training rows and ask the model to fit them perfectly. A net that cannot reach perfect accuracy on data it is allowed to memorize is broken: the learning rate, the initialization, or the loss is wrong. Only after that fit works is a gap between train and validation evidence of overfitting.

Accuracy hides a rare class. Precision is the fraction of predicted positives that are positive. Recall is the fraction of real positives the model found. A model that labels everything negative scores a high accuracy on a rare event and a recall of zero.
